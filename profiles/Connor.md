## Writing a proposal for Connor

1. **Lead with production against prototype.** His own framing, "not just getting a demo to work",
   and it answers the fear most of these clients carry.
2. **Pick the reference by shape, not industry.** Juricratic for RAG, retrieval, knowledge graphs,
   agents and anything needing provenance or human review. QuickWMS for large self-built
   operational platforms. QuickVisionz for vision, hardware, edge and latency. Upscaled and CPO
   Media for ecommerce, inventory and growth.
3. **He has run the business, not only built for one.** On operational tooling, inventory or
   marketplace jobs, that is the half most engineers bidding will not have.
4. **Human-in-the-loop is his language**, and it appears across all three sources. Use it on any
   agent job where unsupervised action is a liability.
5. **His work pool is a search corpus, not a link, and it is searched first.**
   `connor-work-pool.md` pools 134 works by domain, his repositories merged with his site's
   projects. **Ten carry a verified live demo: send the demo and the source together.** Search it for the shape of the job, read the
   repository before claiming anything about it, and print **one** URL only if it earns its place.
   **If nothing there fits, run the ordinary gate workflow** — public GitHub, publish, hold, write
   from what comes back — rather than writing the proposal without any repository behind it.
   **Never link the account:** 631 of his 755 public repositories are forks, so the profile reads
   against him.
6. **The founder pattern cuts both ways.** Every system described is one he architected or founded.
   There is no evidence of working inside someone else's engineering organisation, taking a ticket,
   or being code-reviewed by a peer. On a job that is explicitly joining a team, expect that to be
   probed.
